- **Allowed to spend:** 0xa152f8bb749c55e9943a3a0a3111d18ee2b3f94e
- **Amount:** unlimited (the maximum possible)
- **Why it was dangerous:**
  - That address has no contract code, so it's a personal wallet, not an app. Real apps like exchanges and lending sites get approvals through contracts.
  - Its address looks like yours (0xa15…) on purpose. This is a common phishing and "address poisoning" trick.
  - It was approved about 36 blocks after you received 1,314.85 USDC. The approval could have let whoever controls that address take your entire balance at any time. That timing and setup fit a scam site or bad signature request.

**What I did**
- I set the approval to 0 (transaction hash 0xb216009ff3c85528c639717e89918e4d50c4507d406e98ae5ccf7119f6c05c48).
- I checked it on-chain afterward: the allowance is now 0.
- Your 1,314.85 USDC is still in your wallet.

**What to do next**
- Don't copy or send to 0xa152…f94e, and always check the full address before sending.
- If you don't remember approving this, think about what site you connected to or signed something on around then. Treat that site as a phishing site.
- If you think your seed phrase or private key may have leaked, move your funds to a new wallet.
